# Answering consistently and honestly

Search for the OPQ and you will find plenty of advice on how to "beat" it: work out the ideal profile for the job and answer to fit. This page explains why that is a poor bet, what to do instead, and how to prepare for the part that really matters, which is talking about your profile afterwards.

## Why "answering to fit" is a poor bet

Four reasons, each from SHL's own material or from how the process works:

1. **The format is built against it.** SHL says the OPQ shows three equally desirable statements so that it is not clear which one would be considered "best", and that this is designed to reduce faking. You cannot simply agree with every flattering statement, because every choice costs another.
2. **The report shows consistency.** SHL's sample OPQ Profile includes a Consistency scale showing whether someone "has responded more consistently across the questionnaire". Answering some blocks as yourself and others as an imagined ideal tends to work against that.
3. **The profile gets discussed.** SHL lists improving interview hit rates among the OPQ's uses. A profile that says you love negotiating invites "tell me about a negotiation you enjoyed". If the honest answer is that you avoid them, the gap shows.
4. **Fit cuts both ways.** If you squeeze into a profile for a role that does not suit how you work, the reward is a job that does not suit how you work.

You do not need to be harsh on yourself either. Describe how you behave at work in an ordinary week, as a colleague who knows you well would.

## What "consistent" means here

Choosing the same scale every time is impossible, because a scale meets different partners in different blocks and its rank moves with them. Consistency here means **answering every block from the same picture of yourself**. If you are a careful planner, you are a careful planner in block 12 and in block 87, whatever else is in the block.

Three habits help:

| Habit | How |
|---|---|
| Fix your reference point | Before you start, pick one context: your current job, your last placement, or your studies if you have no work experience. Use it for every block. |
| Use real examples | For each choice, think of something you actually did. Behaviour is steadier than mood. |
| Keep the same version of you | Picture an ordinary week, the kind you have most often. |

## Before the questionnaire: know yourself on paper

This is the useful preparation, and it takes about an hour.

1. **Read the three area guides** and their scale tables: [Relationships with People](sections/relationships-with-people.md), [Thinking Style](sections/thinking-style.md), [Feelings and Emotions](sections/feelings-and-emotions.md).
2. **Mark each of the 32 scales high, middle or low** for yourself, honestly.
3. **For your five highest and five lowest, write one real example each.** A time you took charge. A time you left the detail to someone else.
4. **Ask someone who works with you** to look at your list. If they disagree strongly on a scale, think about why.
5. **Then do the questionnaire** without looking at the list. The list is for understanding yourself and for interview, not for steering answers.

## After the questionnaire: prepare to talk about it

If the employer uses your profile at interview, the questions usually probe your extremes. Prepare for both ends.

| If your profile is high on | Expect a question like | A good answer shows |
|---|---|---|
| Independent Minded | "Tell me about a time you went against the team." | Why you did it, and how you handled the people side |
| Decisive | "When has a quick decision gone wrong?" | That you learn and know when to slow down |
| Detail Conscious | "How do you handle a deadline that does not leave time to check everything?" | That you can prioritise |

| If your profile is low on | Expect a question like | A good answer shows |
|---|---|---|
| Socially Confident | "This role involves meeting clients. How do you approach that?" | What you do to prepare, and an example where it went well |
| Forward Thinking | "How do you plan your work?" | The methods you use, even if planning is not your first instinct |
| Tough-minded | "How do you take critical feedback?" | That feedback affects you and how you use it |

Every profile has low scores. Interviewers expect them, and a clear example of how you work with a low score usually lands better than a claim that it does not matter.

## Questions people ask

**Can I fail the OPQ?** There is no pass mark. An employer may decide your profile does not fit a particular role, which is a judgement about fit, not a failure.

**Should I answer as I am at work or as I am at home?** At work. SHL's example page describes the statements as about behaviour at work.

**What if I have little work experience?** Use your studies, a part-time job, volunteering or a team activity as your reference point, and keep to it.

**Does SHL detect cheating?** SHL says the forced-choice format is designed to reduce faking, and its sample profile includes a consistency scale. SHL does not publish more detail than that, so be wary of any site that claims to know the exact checks.

## Practise answering steadily

Try the free blocks on the [SHL OPQ32 practice page on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/shl-opq32?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=cu2th7&utm_content=shl-opq32r) using one fixed reference point, and its free 55-item trait profiler. Then compare what they show with your own high-middle-low list. Where they disagree, ask which one is closer to the truth.
