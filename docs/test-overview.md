# The SHL OPQ32r explained

The OPQ32r is SHL's Occupational Personality Questionnaire, the "r" version. It asks how you see your own behaviour at work and turns your answers into a profile across 32 characteristics, from how persuasive you are to how much you plan ahead. Employers use it in hiring, especially for graduate, professional and management roles, and in development and team building. There is no pass mark and no right answers. This page covers what it is and how it works.

## Quick facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Publisher | SHL |
| Version | OPQ32r, which SHL calls the latest and most comprehensive version of the OPQ |
| Questions | 104 |
| Format | Forced choice: you choose between statements rather than rating each one |
| Time | Untimed. SHL's figures for average completion are 15 to 25 minutes (fact sheet, 2022) and about 20 minutes (product page). |
| Sittings | One |
| Where | Online, designed for unproctored use and built mobile-first |
| Languages | 37, per SHL's product page |
| What it measures | 32 personality characteristics at work |
| Pass mark | None. SHL describes personality questionnaires as having no right or wrong answers. |

Sources: [SHL OPQ fact sheet, 2022](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf), [SHL OPQ product page](https://www.shl.com/products/assessments/personality-assessment/shl-occupational-personality-questionnaire-opq/) and [SHL Direct example questions](https://www.shl.com/shldirect/en/example-questions/personality-questionnaire/), checked 24 November 2026.

## The 32 characteristics

SHL's fact sheet lists the 32 characteristics under three broad areas. Each area has its own guide in this repo.

| Area | Sub-groups | Scales | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relationships with People | Influence, Sociability, Empathy | 10 | [Relationships with People](sections/relationships-with-people.md) |
| Thinking Style | Analysis, Creativity and Change, Structure | 12 | [Thinking Style](sections/thinking-style.md) |
| Feelings and Emotions | Emotions, Dynamism | 10 | [Feelings and Emotions](sections/feelings-and-emotions.md) |

SHL's psychometric testing page describes the model as three domains, Relationships with People, Thinking Styles, and Feelings and Emotions, with Dynamism as a possible fourth. The fact sheet places Dynamism under Feelings and Emotions, and so do we.

## How the questionnaire works

SHL's example page describes the format: you see three statements about behaviour at work, choose the one most like you, that statement disappears, and you choose the more like you of the two that remain. SHL says the statements in each set are designed to be equally desirable, which makes it hard to pick a "best" answer and is meant to reduce faking. See [how the forced-choice format works](forced-choice-format.md) for the detail.

SHL lists 104 questions, and its example page shows each one as a set of three statements.

## Who uses it and why

SHL's fact sheet lists uses including choosing applicants for a role, improving interview hit rates, identifying future leaders and building project teams. In hiring, your profile is usually compared with what the role needs, often through competencies. SHL's fact sheet says the 32 characteristics can be mapped to the competencies a job requires.

In plain terms, an employer is not looking for a "good personality". It is asking whether your way of working fits what the role involves, and what to explore at interview.

## OPQ32r and OPQ32n

You may see two versions mentioned:

| Version | Format | Questions | SHL's guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| OPQ32r | Forced choice | 104 | The latest version, recommended for selection |
| OPQ32n | Normative: rate each statement on a five-point scale | 230 | SHL's fact sheet says that for selection and high-stakes use the recommendation is the OPQ32r |

Sources: [OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf), [OPQ32n fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ32n_Fact_Sheet.pdf).

## The one habit that runs through the whole questionnaire

Answer about how you actually behave at work, on a normal week, not how you think the employer wants you to be. The format is built to make "ideal candidate" answering hard, your profile may be discussed at interview, and a role that suits a made-up profile may not suit you. The [answering honestly](answering-honestly.md) page explains why this is also the practical choice.

## Practise the format

The format feels odd the first time, because every block forces a choice between things that all sound reasonable. Try it before the real one: the free [SHL OPQ32 practice page on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/shl-opq32?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=shl-opq32r) lets you try forced-choice blocks and see a sample result, and has a free 55-item trait profiler. SHL also hosts [example questions](https://www.shl.com/shldirect/en/example-questions/personality-questionnaire/).
