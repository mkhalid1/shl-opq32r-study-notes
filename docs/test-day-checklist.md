# Test-day checklist

The OPQ32r is untimed and taken online, usually on your own. That makes it easy to take at a bad moment. Most of this checklist is about picking a good one.

## The day before

- [ ] Read the invitation again: the deadline and whether other tests come in the same link.
- [ ] If you need an adjustment, ask the employer before you start.
- [ ] Decide your reference point: your current job, your last placement, or your studies. Write it down.
- [ ] Skim your high-middle-low list from [answering honestly](answering-honestly.md#before-the-questionnaire-know-yourself-on-paper) once, then put it away.

## Just before you start

- [ ] Choose a time when you are rested and not upset or rushed. Allow 30 minutes, even though SHL's average is 15 to 25.
- [ ] Find a quiet place. SHL says the OPQ was designed mobile-first, so a phone is fine, but a calm setting matters more than the device.
- [ ] Turn off notifications.
- [ ] Remind yourself: no right answers, no pass mark, a normal week at work.

## During the questionnaire

- [ ] Read all three statements before choosing.
- [ ] Choose the one most like you. Then the more like you of the remaining two.
- [ ] Think of real behaviour, not how you would like to be.
- [ ] If stuck for 30 seconds, take your first reaction and move on.
- [ ] Keep the same reference point for every block.

## Afterwards

- [ ] Note any blocks that felt hard. They often point to scales worth an interview example.
- [ ] If the employer sends a report, read it against the scale tables in this repo.
- [ ] Prepare for interview questions about your highest and lowest scales.
- [ ] If something went wrong technically, contact the employer or SHL's candidate support with details.

## A practice run first

If you have never seen forced-choice blocks, do a practice run before the real one. The free [SHL OPQ32 practice page on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/shl-opq32?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=shl-opq32r) lets you try forced-choice blocks of three statements. Its blocks ask for a most-like and a least-like choice, the older OPQ style. The ranking you end up with is the same as in the current choose-then-choose-again format.
