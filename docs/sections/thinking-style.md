# Thinking Style

**In one line:** twelve OPQ scales about how you approach work and problems: how you analyse, how you handle ideas and change, and how you plan and organise.

Thinking Style is the second broad area of the OPQ32, with three sub-groups: **Analysis**, **Creativity and Change** and **Structure**. It is the largest area, with twelve of the 32 scales. This page explains each one in plain words and walks through five example blocks.

## What it measures

These scales describe how you prefer to think and work, as you see it. They are not an intelligence test: someone who prefers proven methods is not less able than someone who loves new ideas. Many jobs need both kinds of people, and the employer decides which matters for the role.

| Sub-group | Scale | Higher scores describe someone who | Lower scores describe someone who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis | **Data Rational** | enjoys data and wants evidence before deciding | goes more by judgement and how people feel about an option |
| Analysis | **Evaluative** | examines information critically and looks for weak points | is less inclined to pick plans and arguments apart |
| Analysis | **Behavioural** | tries to understand why people act as they do | takes people's behaviour at face value |
| Creativity and Change | **Conventional** | prefers tried and tested ways of working | is happy to drop the usual method |
| Creativity and Change | **Conceptual** | enjoys theory and abstract discussion | prefers practical, concrete problems |
| Creativity and Change | **Innovative** | generates new ideas and original solutions | tends to build on other people's ideas |
| Creativity and Change | **Variety Seeking** | wants variety and gets bored by routine | is comfortable with routine and repetition |
| Creativity and Change | **Adaptable** | changes approach to suit different people and situations | behaves much the same way whatever the situation |
| Structure | **Forward Thinking** | plans ahead and thinks about the long term | focuses on the here and now |
| Structure | **Detail Conscious** | is careful and attentive to small details | is less concerned with fine detail |
| Structure | **Conscientious** | sees tasks through to completion and keeps deadlines | is more flexible about finishing every task |
| Structure | **Rule Following** | sticks to rules and procedures | is willing to bend rules when they get in the way |

The scale names come from SHL's [OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf). The descriptions are our own summaries.

## How the statements look

You see three statements at a time and choose the one most like you, then the more like you of the remaining two. SHL's example page describes this "select, then select again" flow. See [how the forced-choice format works](../forced-choice-format.md).

In this area, statements often sound equally sensible, for example "I plan my work weeks ahead" and "I check my work for small errors". Both are good habits. The block asks which one you actually do *more*.

## A method that works

1. **Read all three statements first.**
2. **Picture a recent piece of work.** A project, a report, an assignment. What did you actually spend your time on?
3. **Rank by what you do, not what you approve of.** Most people approve of planning ahead. Fewer actually do it every week.
4. **Use a tie-breaker.** "What would a colleague say I am known for?" or "What do I do first when I start a new task?"
5. **Keep work and hobbies apart.** You may love experimenting in the kitchen and still prefer proven methods at work. The OPQ asks about work.
6. **Answer and move on.**

## Traps that cost accuracy

- **Treating every structure statement as a must.** Ranking Detail Conscious, Conscientious and Rule Following first every time because they sound professional describes someone more structured than you are. That is inaccurate, and it can steer you toward roles that expect a level of procedure you would find tiring.
- **Confusing Innovative with Variety Seeking.** Coming up with new ideas is not the same as wanting your days to differ.
- **Confusing Conventional with Rule Following.** Preferring proven methods is about how you solve problems. Following rules is about procedures and policy.
- **Confusing Evaluative with Data Rational.** You can critique an argument closely without using a single number.
- **Answering about the role you want rather than the one you do.** If you are moving from operations into strategy, answer about how you work now. The interview is where you explain your direction.

## Timing

The questionnaire is untimed, and SHL's figures for average completion range from 15 to 25 minutes. As our guidance: around 10 to 15 seconds per block keeps you honest and steady.

## Practice items

These five example blocks are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the OPQ32r or any SHL material. For each block:

1. **Name the scale** each statement points to, using the table above. This is scored below.
2. **Rank the statements for yourself.** This is not scored. The explanations show how different, honest people might rank it.

### Block 1

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I like working with numbers and statistics. |
| B | I plan my work weeks in advance. |
| C | I come up with original ideas. |

### Block 2

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I look for the weak points in a plan before I agree to it. |
| B | I prefer to use methods that have worked before. |
| C | I like my working days to be different from one another. |

### Block 3

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I try to work out why people behave the way they do. |
| B | I check my work carefully for small errors. |
| C | I adjust how I come across depending on who I am dealing with. |

### Block 4

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I enjoy discussing theories and abstract ideas. |
| B | I see every task through to the end, even the dull ones. |
| C | I follow procedures even when they slow me down. |

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, blocks 1 to 4</summary>

**Block 1: A Data Rational, B Forward Thinking, C Innovative.** A is about working with figures. B is about planning ahead. C is about generating new ideas. *How two people might rank it:* a finance graduate who builds spreadsheets daily puts A first. A marketing assistant who is always the one suggesting a new campaign angle puts C first. Someone who keeps a weekly plan and actually follows it might put B first, whatever their job.

**Block 2: A Evaluative, B Conventional, C Variety Seeking.** A is about critical examination. B is about preferring proven methods. C is about wanting variety. *How two people might rank it:* a quality engineer who reviews designs for faults puts A first. Someone in a role with a different problem every day who loves that puts C first. Note that B and C can both be true of you at different times. The block asks which is *more* like you.

**Block 3: A Behavioural, B Detail Conscious, C Adaptable.** A is about analysing people's motives. B is about care with detail. C is about changing your style to suit the situation. *How two people might rank it:* a customer service lead who changes tone between an angry caller and a nervous one might put C first. An editor puts B first. If you often find yourself explaining to friends why a colleague acted as they did, A may be your first choice.

**Block 4: A Conceptual, B Conscientious, C Rule Following.** A is about enjoying abstract thinking. B is about finishing what you start. C is about following procedures. *How two people might rank it:* a research student who loves debating ideas puts A first. Someone who has never missed a deadline puts B first. Be careful with C: "even when they slow me down" is the honest test. If you sometimes skip a step to get things done, rank it lower.

</details>

### Block 5

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I think months ahead about what my work will need. |
| B | I enjoy working through spreadsheets and statistics. |
| C | I look for faults in arguments, including my own. |

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, block 5</summary>

**Block 5: A Forward Thinking, B Data Rational, C Evaluative.** A is about planning ahead. B is about evidence from figures. C is about critical evaluation. *How two people might rank it:* B and C are both "analytical" but differ. B is about numbers. C is about the logic of an argument. A data analyst might put B first. A policy officer reviewing proposals might put C first.

## Score yourself

This scores your **scale recognition**. Your own rankings are not scored, because there are no right or wrong answers.

| Block | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data Rational | Forward Thinking | Innovative |
| 2 | Evaluative | Conventional | Variety Seeking |
| 3 | Behavioural | Detail Conscious | Adaptable |
| 4 | Conceptual | Conscientious | Rule Following |
| 5 | Forward Thinking | Data Rational | Evaluative |

Count your correct scale names out of 15.

- **13 to 15:** solid. Move to [Feelings and Emotions](feelings-and-emotions.md).
- **10 to 12:** the common mix-ups are Innovative with Variety Seeking, Conventional with Rule Following, and Evaluative with Data Rational. Reread those rows.
- **9 or fewer:** reread the table, then redo the blocks naming each scale before ranking.
