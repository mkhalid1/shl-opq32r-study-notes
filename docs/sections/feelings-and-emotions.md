# Feelings and Emotions

**In one line:** ten OPQ scales about how you handle pressure, setbacks and other people's motives, and how much drive and pace you bring to work.

SHL's fact sheet puts two sub-groups under Feelings and Emotions: **Emotions**, with six scales, and **Dynamism**, with four. SHL's psychometric testing page describes Dynamism as a possible fourth domain alongside the other three. This page treats them together, as the fact sheet does.

## What it measures

These scales describe how you see your own reactions and energy at work. They are often the ones candidates feel most tempted to "improve", because words like Worrying sound negative. They are not judgements. A person who worries before important events often prepares more thoroughly. A person who is very relaxed may need more prompting about risks. The employer decides which matters for the role.

| Sub-group | Scale | Higher scores describe someone who | Lower scores describe someone who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotions | **Relaxed** | feels calm and rarely tense day to day | tends to feel tense or on edge more often |
| Emotions | **Worrying** | gets nervous before important events | stays calm before big occasions |
| Emotions | **Tough-minded** | is not easily hurt by criticism | is sensitive to criticism and insults |
| Emotions | **Optimistic** | expects things to turn out well | tends to expect problems |
| Emotions | **Trusting** | takes people at their word | is wary of other people's motives |
| Emotions | **Emotionally Controlled** | keeps feelings to themselves | shows how they feel |
| Dynamism | **Vigorous** | likes to keep busy and has lots of energy | prefers a steadier pace |
| Dynamism | **Competitive** | wants to win and to beat others | is less bothered about winning |
| Dynamism | **Achieving** | sets demanding goals and is ambitious | is less driven by targets and career goals |
| Dynamism | **Decisive** | makes decisions quickly | takes time to weigh things up before deciding |

The scale names come from SHL's [OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf). The descriptions are our own summaries.

## How the statements look

Three statements per block, as in the rest of the questionnaire. You choose the one most like you, then the more like you of the remaining two. See [how the forced-choice format works](../forced-choice-format.md).

In this area a block may put a positive-sounding statement next to one that sounds less flattering, such as "I feel nervous before a big presentation". SHL says blocks are designed so the statements are equally desirable. In practice that means no single statement is the safe one to pick, so the only sensible strategy is to pick what is true.

## A method that works

1. **Read all three statements first.**
2. **Think of the last stressful week at work or in your studies.** Exams, a deadline, a difficult client. How did you actually react?
3. **Separate what you feel from what you show.** You can feel nervous inside and still look calm. Worrying is about the feeling. Emotionally Controlled is about showing it.
4. **For Dynamism, think about a normal week, not a crunch.** Everyone is busy before a deadline. Vigorous is about your usual pace.
5. **Use a tie-breaker.** "What would someone who has worked with me for a year say?"
6. **Answer and move on.** Long thought on these items tends to push answers toward an ideal self.

## Traps that cost accuracy

- **Hiding every sign of nerves.** Ranking every Worrying statement last when you do get nervous describes a calmer person than you are. That is simply inaccurate, and it leaves you without an honest answer if you are asked how you handle pressure.
- **Confusing Relaxed with Worrying.** They are separate scales. You can be calm most days and still get nervous before big events.
- **Confusing Competitive with Achieving.** Wanting to beat others is not the same as setting yourself high targets.
- **Confusing Decisive with Controlling.** Deciding quickly is about speed. Controlling, in Relationships with People, is about directing others.
- **Answering from a bad day.** If you take the questionnaire after a rejection or a rough week, you may rate yourself less optimistic than you usually are. Answer about your typical week, not today, and if you can, choose a calm time to sit it.

## Timing

Untimed, with SHL's average completion figures between 15 and 25 minutes. As our guidance: 10 to 15 seconds per block. These items feel personal, so give yourself a moment, then commit.

## Practice items

These five example blocks are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the OPQ32r or any SHL material. For each block:

1. **Name the scale** each statement points to, using the table above. This is scored below.
2. **Rank the statements for yourself.** This is not scored. The explanations show how two different, honest people might rank it.

### Block 1

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I rarely feel tense, even on busy days. |
| B | I like to keep busy all the time. |
| C | I make up my mind quickly. |

### Block 2

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I feel nervous before an important presentation. |
| B | I want to come out on top when I am compared with others. |
| C | I usually believe what people tell me. |

### Block 3

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | Criticism of my work does not upset me much. |
| B | I set myself demanding targets. |
| C | I keep my feelings to myself at work. |

### Block 4

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I expect things to work out well, even when a project is behind. |
| B | I make decisions without needing a lot of time to think. |
| C | I prefer a packed schedule to a quiet one. |

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, blocks 1 to 4</summary>

**Block 1: A Relaxed, B Vigorous, C Decisive.** A is about general calm. B is about energy and keeping busy. C is about deciding fast. *How two people might rank it:* a hospital porter who loves a full shift puts B first. A team leader who is known for quick calls puts C first. If you are calm most of the time, A may come first, even if you are also busy.

**Block 2: A Worrying, B Competitive, C Trusting.** A is about nerves before big events. B is about wanting to win. C is about taking people at their word. *How two people might rank it:* this is the kind of block where people are tempted to rank A last automatically. If you do get nervous before presentations, and many capable people do, A can honestly come first. Someone on a sales leaderboard who checks their ranking every morning would put B first.

**Block 3: A Tough-minded, B Achieving, C Emotionally Controlled.** A is about not being hurt by criticism. B is about ambition and targets. C is about not showing feelings. *How two people might rank it:* a graduate who sets a promotion goal for every year puts B first. Someone who stays expressionless in tense meetings puts C first. A and C are easy to mix up. A is about how criticism lands inside. C is about what others can see.

**Block 4: A Optimistic, B Decisive, C Vigorous.** A is about expecting good outcomes. B is about quick decisions. C is about preferring a busy pace. *How two people might rank it:* someone who keeps the team's spirits up when a deadline slips puts A first. Someone who gets restless on a slow day puts C first.

</details>

### Block 5

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | Harsh feedback does not stay with me for long. |
| B | I usually assume a setback will be temporary. |
| C | I am full of energy at work. |

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, block 5</summary>

**Block 5: A Tough-minded, B Optimistic, C Vigorous.** A is about resilience to criticism. B is about expecting good outcomes. C is about energy. *How two people might rank it:* A and B can feel similar but are different. Tough-minded is about criticism of you. Optimistic is about how you expect events to go.

## Score yourself

This scores your **scale recognition**. Your own rankings are not scored.

| Block | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relaxed | Vigorous | Decisive |
| 2 | Worrying | Competitive | Trusting |
| 3 | Tough-minded | Achieving | Emotionally Controlled |
| 4 | Optimistic | Decisive | Vigorous |
| 5 | Tough-minded | Optimistic | Vigorous |

Count your correct scale names out of 15.

- **13 to 15:** you can read this part of a profile easily. Go on to [how results are reported](../scoring.md).
- **10 to 12:** the common mix-ups are Relaxed with Worrying, Tough-minded with Emotionally Controlled, and Competitive with Achieving. Reread those rows.
- **9 or fewer:** reread the table, then redo the blocks naming each scale first.

### Your own pattern

Which of these scales did you rank first most often, and which last? The ones you put last are as useful as the ones you put first. If you rarely chose Decisive, be ready to describe how you make careful decisions. If you often chose Worrying, be ready to describe how you prepare. See [answering honestly](../answering-honestly.md).
