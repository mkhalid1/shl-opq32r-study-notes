# Relationships with People

**In one line:** ten OPQ scales about how you deal with other people at work: how much you lead and persuade, how sociable you are, and how much you consult and support others.

SHL groups the OPQ32's 32 characteristics into broad areas. Relationships with People is the first, with three sub-groups: **Influence**, **Sociability** and **Empathy**. This page explains each scale in plain words, shows how statements about them appear in a block, and walks through five example blocks.

## What it measures

These scales describe your preferred style with people at work, as you see it yourself. They are not a test of social skill, and a high score is not better than a low one. A strong salesperson and a strong auditor usually look very different here, and both can be excellent at their jobs.

| Sub-group | Scale | Higher scores describe someone who | Lower scores describe someone who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Influence | **Persuasive** | likes to bring others round to a view, enjoys selling and negotiating | prefers not to push people to change their minds |
| Influence | **Controlling** | likes to take the lead and direct others | is happy for someone else to run things |
| Influence | **Outspoken** | says what they think, including disagreement | tends to keep critical views to themselves |
| Influence | **Independent Minded** | follows their own approach even against the majority | goes along with the group's decision |
| Sociability | **Outgoing** | is lively and talkative in groups | is quieter and more reserved in groups |
| Sociability | **Affiliative** | enjoys company at work and prefers working in a team | is comfortable working alone for long stretches |
| Sociability | **Socially Confident** | feels at ease meeting new people and in formal settings | can feel awkward with strangers or on formal occasions |
| Empathy | **Modest** | rarely talks about their own successes | is comfortable making achievements known |
| Empathy | **Democratic** | consults widely before deciding | is willing to decide alone |
| Empathy | **Caring** | is supportive and gets involved in others' problems | keeps a professional distance from others' personal issues |

The scale names come from SHL's [OPQ fact sheet](https://service.shl.com/docs/OPQ%20Product%20Fact%20Sheet%20(1).pdf). The descriptions are our own summaries.

## How the statements look

In the OPQ32r you see three statements at a time. SHL's example page says you pick the one most like you, it disappears, and then you pick the one of the remaining two that is most like you. See [how the forced-choice format works](../forced-choice-format.md).

Statements are short, first-person and about behaviour at work, for example (our own wording): "I enjoy winning people round to my point of view." Each statement in a block points to a different scale, and SHL says the three are designed to be equally desirable, so there is no obviously "right" one to pick.

## A method that works

The goal is an accurate picture of how you work, so the method is about honesty and consistency, not about any target profile.

1. **Read all three statements before choosing.** You are ranking them against each other, not rating each one alone.
2. **Think of a real week at work or study.** Not your best day, not how you would like to be.
3. **Ask "which of these did I actually do more often?"** Behaviour you can remember beats a general feeling about yourself.
4. **If two feel equal, use a tie-breaker question.** "Which would people who work with me say first?" usually settles it.
5. **Do not hunt for the scale.** Knowing that a statement points to Persuasive helps you understand the report later. While answering, just judge the behaviour.
6. **Move on.** Your first honest judgement is usually right. Long deliberation tends to pull answers toward an ideal self.

## Traps that cost accuracy

- **Answering as the ideal candidate.** Picking "I take charge" every time because the job ad says "leader" gives a profile that does not match you. It misdescribes you, which is the real problem: the employer learns about someone who is not you, and you may be judged against a role that does not suit how you work.
- **Thinking of home, not work.** You may be the quiet one at family events and the voice of the team at work. The OPQ asks about work.
- **Letting one memorable event decide.** One great negotiation does not make you someone who enjoys negotiating every week.
- **Reading "Modest" as weak or "Outspoken" as rude.** Both are styles. Neither is a flaw on its own.
- **Ranking by what sounds polite.** "I ask for others' views" sounds nicer than "I decide quickly on my own", but if you usually decide alone, say so.

## Timing

The OPQ32r is untimed. SHL's figures for the average completion time range from 15 to 25 minutes, and its product page says about 20 minutes. As our guidance: aim for around 10 to 15 seconds per block. You need enough time to read all three statements, not enough to start constructing a version of yourself.

## Practice items

These five example blocks are original, written for these notes. They are not taken from the OPQ32r or any SHL material. Each block has two tasks:

1. **Name the scale** each statement points to, using the table above. This has a right answer and is scored below.
2. **Rank the statements for yourself.** This has no right answer. Pick the one most like you at work, then the more like you of the remaining two. The explanations show how two different, honest people might rank the same block.

### Block 1

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I say so when I disagree with someone, even a senior colleague. |
| B | I feel at ease walking into a room full of people I have never met. |
| C | I make time to help colleagues who are dealing with personal problems. |

### Block 2

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I like to be the one who directs what a group does. |
| B | I keep quiet about my own successes. |
| C | I am lively and talkative in team meetings. |

### Block 3

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I stick with my own approach even when most of the team prefers another. |
| B | I involve the whole team when we have a decision to make. |
| C | I enjoy negotiating a better deal. |

### Block 4

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I would rather work alongside others than on my own. |
| B | I feel comfortable at formal business dinners. |
| C | I tell people clearly when I think their idea will not work. |

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, blocks 1 to 4</summary>

**Block 1: A Outspoken, B Socially Confident, C Caring.** A is about voicing disagreement. B is about ease with strangers. C is about supporting others with personal matters. *How two people might rank it:* someone who regularly challenges plans in meetings but dreads networking events would put A first and B last. Someone who is the first person colleagues come to when life gets hard would put C first. If you cannot decide, ask which one your manager would mention first.

**Block 2: A Controlling, B Modest, C Outgoing.** A is about taking the lead. B is about not advertising achievements. C is about energy in groups. *How two people might rank it:* a team captain type puts A first. A quietly effective analyst who never mentions a win in the team chat puts B first. Note that C and A are different things: you can be lively in meetings without wanting to run them, or run a team in a calm, quiet way.

**Block 3: A Independent Minded, B Democratic, C Persuasive.** A is about holding your own course against the majority. B is about consulting widely. C is about selling and negotiating. *How two people might rank it:* this block often feels uncomfortable because A and B pull in opposite directions. That is the point of the format: it makes you choose. Think of the last real disagreement about how to do something. Did you go your own way, or bring everyone in? Rank from that memory.

**Block 4: A Affiliative, B Socially Confident, C Outspoken.** A is about preferring company at work. B is about ease in formal settings. C is about voicing criticism. *How two people might rank it:* someone who loves an open-plan office but finds formal dinners stiff would rank A, then C or B depending on how often they push back on ideas. Be careful not to merge A and B: liking people around you is not the same as feeling at ease with strangers in formal settings.

</details>

### Block 5

| | Statement |
|---|---|
| A | I am usually one of the more animated people in a group. |
| B | I am prepared to go against a decision the majority has made. |
| C | I like changing people's minds. |

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, block 5</summary>

**Block 5: A Outgoing, B Independent Minded, C Persuasive.** A is about liveliness in groups. B is about acting against the majority. C is about changing views. *How two people might rank it:* B and C can look alike, but they are different. Persuasive people work to bring others round. Independent Minded people may simply carry on their own way without trying to convince anyone. Think about which you actually do.

</details>

## Score yourself

This scores your **scale recognition**, which helps you read an OPQ report and prepare for interview questions about it. Your personal rankings are not scored, because there are no right or wrong answers.

| Block | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Outspoken | Socially Confident | Caring |
| 2 | Controlling | Modest | Outgoing |
| 3 | Independent Minded | Democratic | Persuasive |
| 4 | Affiliative | Socially Confident | Outspoken |
| 5 | Outgoing | Independent Minded | Persuasive |

Count your correct scale names out of 15.

- **13 to 15:** you know these scales well. Move to [Thinking Style](thinking-style.md).
- **10 to 12:** the usual mix-ups are Persuasive with Independent Minded, Affiliative with Socially Confident, and Outgoing with Controlling. Reread their rows in the table.
- **9 or fewer:** read the table once more, then redo the blocks naming the scale before you look at your own ranking.

Look at your rankings across the five blocks. Which two or three scales did you put first most often? Write one real work example for each.
