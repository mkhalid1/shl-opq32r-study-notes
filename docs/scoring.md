# How OPQ32r results are reported

Short version: your choices are turned into a score on each of the 32 scales, each score is compared with a group of other people, and the result is shown as a sten from 1 to 10. There is no pass or fail. The employer reads the profile against what the role needs, and often uses it to plan interview questions.

## Stens on a 1 to 10 scale

SHL's sample OPQ Profile shows each of the 32 scales on a sten scale from 1 to 10, with a short description of the low end on the left and the high end on the right.

| Sten | Rough meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 | Lower than most of the comparison group on this scale |
| 4 to 7 | Around the middle of the group |
| 8 to 10 | Higher than most of the group |

That reading is a general guide to stens, not SHL wording. The key point is that every score is **relative**. A sten of 3 on Outgoing does not mean you are unsociable. It means you described yourself as less outgoing than most people in the comparison group did.

## The comparison group

SHL's sample profile names the questionnaire version and the comparison group used, for example a general population group. Your raw choices mean little until they are set against a group. The employer or SHL picks the group, and it can differ by country, language and role level.

## The consistency scale

SHL's sample OPQ Profile includes a **Consistency** scale alongside the 32 characteristics, with "has responded less consistently across the questionnaire" at one end and "has responded more consistently across the questionnaire" at the other.

What this means for you: the report shows whether your answers hang together. SHL does not publish how the consistency scale is calculated or what employers should do with a low score, so we do not guess. The practical advice is the same either way. Answer every block from the same honest picture of yourself, and the question does not arise. See [answering honestly](answering-honestly.md).

## Reports employers and candidates may see

SHL's fact sheet lists a range of reports for HR professionals, line managers and candidates, for recruitment, development and team building. Examples from SHL's sample documents:

| Report | Who it is for | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| OPQ Profile | Trained users | Stens for all 32 scales and the consistency scale |
| OPQ Candidate Report | You | A plain-language summary under the three broad areas |
| Competency-based reports | Employers | Your profile mapped to job competencies |

Whether you receive a report depends on the employer. SHL's sample Candidate Report says it is based on your own view of your behaviour, compares your answers with a large group, and that whoever gives you feedback should be qualified to explain it.

## How long a profile stays relevant

SHL's sample Candidate Report says the description is likely to be a good picture of your behaviour at work for about 18 to 24 months, depending on your role and circumstances. People do change, especially early in their careers.

## How employers use the profile

SHL's fact sheet says the 32 characteristics can be mapped to the competencies of a job role, and lists uses such as identifying best-fit applicants and improving interview hit rates. In practice that tends to mean:

- comparing your profile with the behaviours the role needs
- choosing interview questions that probe your strongest and weakest areas
- combining the profile with ability tests, interviews and other evidence

The questionnaire is one input. SHL's own sample reports stress that the description is a self-report.

## There is no "good" profile

There is no published pass mark, no ideal profile, and no total score. A profile that suits a sales role may suit an audit role badly, and the other way round. The only profile that helps you is the one that describes you, because that is the one you can talk about with confidence at interview.
