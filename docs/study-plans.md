# Study plans

You do not study for a personality questionnaire the way you study for an ability test. You prepare by understanding the format, understanding the 32 scales, and getting clear on how you actually work, so that you answer steadily and can talk about the result. Both plans start with the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md).

## 7-day plan

About 30 to 45 minutes a day.

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The questionnaire | Read the [test overview](test-overview.md) and [how the forced-choice format works](forced-choice-format.md). Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | Relationships with People | Read the [guide](sections/relationships-with-people.md). Do the five example blocks: name each scale, then rank for yourself. |
| 3 | Thinking Style | Read the [guide](sections/thinking-style.md) and do its five blocks. |
| 4 | Feelings and Emotions | Read the [guide](sections/feelings-and-emotions.md) and do its five blocks. |
| 5 | Know yourself on paper | Follow the steps in [answering honestly](answering-honestly.md#before-the-questionnaire-know-yourself-on-paper): mark all 32 scales high, middle or low, and write examples for your top and bottom five. |
| 6 | Practice run | Try the free blocks and the 55-item trait profiler on the [SHL OPQ32 practice page on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/shl-opq32?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=shl-opq32r) with one fixed reference point. Compare what they show with your day 5 list. Read [how results are reported](scoring.md). |
| 7 | Interview prep and rest | Prepare answers for the interview questions in [answering honestly](answering-honestly.md#after-the-questionnaire-prepare-to-talk-about-it). Read the [checklist](test-day-checklist.md). Take the real questionnaire when you are rested. |

### How to review a practice profile

For each scale where the practice profile surprised you, ask:

1. **Is it right?** Sometimes a profile spots something you had not put into words.
2. **If not, what did I do in the blocks?** Did you switch reference points, or answer as you would like to be?
3. **What example would I give at interview?** If you cannot think of one, the scale is worth more thought.

## 2-day plan

For when the link has arrived.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Day 1, first hour | Read [how the forced-choice format works](forced-choice-format.md) and take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| Day 1, second hour | Skim the three scale tables. Do five example blocks from the area you knew least. |
| Day 1, evening | Mark all 32 scales high, middle or low for yourself. Write examples for your top three and bottom three. |
| Day 2 | Choose a calm time with no interruptions. Fix your reference point. Take the real questionnaire. Afterwards, prepare interview examples for your extremes. |

## What not to do

- **Do not try to match a "target profile"** for the job. See [answering honestly](answering-honestly.md).
- **Do not take it straight after bad news** or late at night. Your mood can colour how you rate yourself.
- **Do not overthink single blocks.** One awkward choice does not define your profile.
- **Do not memorise the example blocks.** They are not in the real questionnaire. Their job is to teach the scales.
